# Chaos Experiment 1: Pod Kill

## Setup
- Tool: Chaos Mesh
- Resource: Schedule (kind: Schedule, wrapping PodChaos) — note: newer Chaos Mesh
  versions removed the embedded `spec.scheduler` field on PodChaos directly;
  recurring chaos now requires a separate `Schedule` CRD.
- Action: pod-kill, mode: one
- Interval: every 15s
- Duration of test window: ~65s

## What happened
- 4 separate PodChaos objects were auto-generated by the Schedule during the
  test window (roughly one every 15s), confirming the schedule fired correctly.
- Each triggered a real pod-kill: 4 demo-app pods were deleted and replaced by
  new pods (verified via new pod names/IPs and low pod AGE, e.g. 83s-128s,
  vs. the deployment's original 12h+ uptime).
- RESTARTS stayed at 0 on the new pods — expected, since pod-kill removes the
  whole pod rather than crashing a container in place; the Deployment
  controller creates a brand-new pod to replace it.

## Client impact
Continuous healthcheck loop running against the service during the test:

    TOTAL: success=120 fail=0

Zero failed requests across the full test window despite 4 pod kills.

## Conclusion
With 4 replicas and Service-level load balancing, the demo-app deployment
recovered from random pod kills with zero visible downtime to the client.
This validates that HPA/scheduler + replica count provide real resilience,
not just theoretical redundancy.
